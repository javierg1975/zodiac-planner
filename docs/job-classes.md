# Physical Damage Dealers

They primarily deal physical damage, but some of them have notable utility that make them stand out.

**Physical damage falls behind magic damage if you just buy weapons from the shop, so physical damage generally requires some effort or grinding to make them worthwhile.**

**With endgame weapons and Berserk, endgame physical DPS vastly exceeds magical DPS.**

## Weapon Availability Tiers

Here's a table of some of the noteworthy physical damage weapons that you can get in the early to mid game.

There are more weapons in the early to mid game that you can get as rare drops or steals, but these are the particularly noteworthy or low-effort ones.

For weapon tiers, 1 is the highest tier, with each tier being weaker by about 30%. +/- are used to describe relative placements within a tier. Weapons can move up and down tiers depending on level and equipment. The weapon tiers are mostly determined by when you get them.

With this table, we can divide the physical classes in two categories, based upon their weapon potential:

| **Early to Mid Game**             | **Mid to Late Game**              |
|-----------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Foebreaker                        | Uhlan                             |
| Archer                            | Bushi                             |
| Machinist*                        | Monk                              |
| Shikari**                         | Knight***                         |

*Machinist is a special case because their weapon type, Guns, ignores your stats when dealing damage. As such, they're extremely good in the Early to Mid Game and they can fall off in the Endgame.

**Shikari has a particular endgame setup that can be very damaging, but it's only useful for specific endgame fights.

***Knight is more of an endgame class, but they do have a weapon that they can get in the early to mid game that can work well for a while.

**It's also important to note that although the Late Game physical classes have damage that exceeds the early game classes, Early Game classes still have enough DPS for late game.**

# Spellcasters

These are the Spellcasters:

- **Black Mage** deals great damage and inflicts status effects.

- **White Mage** heals and has good buffs.

- **Red Battlemage** can both deal damage and heal, although their damage tends to be lower than Black Mage's and Red Battlemages don't usually heal very effectively in the late game.

- **Time Battlemage** has some unique utility that is often very useful and early game healing.

- **Monk** and **Knight** both get endgame White Magic, so they can be used in place of White Mage then.

**Unlike Physical Damage Dealers, all of the magic classes are very good throughout the game and fairly low effort to use because you can just buy most spells from the magic shops.**

**Magical DPS falls off at the start of the Great Crystal (~80%) of the game, but magical healing is always relevant throughout the game.**

You can get useful late game spells early if you pick them up from optional dungeons, but even if you don't, mages stay strong throughout the game, unlike Physical Damage Dealers.

**Because these magic classes are so good on their own, pairing any other class with them will be a good combination. Even pairing two magic classes together will be good, although potentially redundant.**

## On MP

MP is not an issue in this game, but running out of MP can be annoying. *Charge* is an ability you get very early on that has a chance to restore MP, but if you fail, you lose all of your MP. The chance of success increases the less MP you have.

In practice, this means that *Charge* allows you to have a minimum of 10% to 20% MP at all times, but having to use *Charge* all of the time can be kind of annoying, as when the gambit AI uses it, they won't move, falling behind the party.

Somewhat weirdly, because there are passives that give you MP when you damage enemies and kill enemies, Red Battlemage and especially Black Mage will often *gain* more MP they use casting damage spells once you get into the mid game. This makes them incredibly self sustainable, so they don't need to use *Charge* very often.

As White Mage does not really deal damage as is, it's fairly common for them to have MP issues. And since damage gives MP, it's incredibly common to add damage to White Mage solving both their damage and MP issues.

Black Magic generally gives better MP regeneration than Physical DPS throughout the game, mostly because MP regeneration is mostly based on the threshold of damage that you've reached per hit, rather than your overall DPS.

## On Time Battlemage

Although I classified Time Battlemage as a Spellcaster, they mostly have utility spells. They are fairly mediocre at both damage and healing, so you can view them as either a poor Physical DPS with extra utility, or a poor Spellcaster with utility.

This doesn't mean that they can't be great, but that it's trickier to pair them into a good combination.

# Specialization over Generalization

In Final Fantasy XII, you generally want to focus your characters as either Physical DPSs or Spellcasters.

This is mostly down to a few reasons:

## Armor

In Final Fantasy XII, there are 3 armor types:

| **Light Armor**       | **Heavy Armor**       | **Mystic Armor**      |
|-----------------------|-----------------------|-----------------------|
| Increases HP          | Increases STR         | Increases MAG and MP  |
| Balanced DEF and MDEF | Higher DEF than MDEF  | Higher MDEF than DEF  |

Heavy Armor and Mystic Armor are very, very important because they increase the character's damage (and healing) stats by a significant amount throughout the entire game.

**This is one of the reasons that contributes to specialization in either physical or magical effectiveness: you get a massive damage and healing boost if you do so.**

**Until your stats are maxed, level appropriate gear adds on average, 25% *each* to a character's effectiveness in either physical damage or magical damage/healer.**

**Combined, the difference between wearing stat appropriate gear is about 50%!**

(For STR weapons with a secondary SPD/MAG/VIT component, the bonus drops down to 15% on average per piece for Physical DPS, which while not *as* huge, is still a large contributor towards DPS.)

## Berserk

***Berserk* is a status effect that multiplies the damage of physical attacks by 1.5x and multiples the speed of your character by 2x. However, in exchange, you will not be able to control the Berserked character, and that character will only attack random enemies.**

**In practice, this means that the character's Physical DPS will be multiplied by 2x, but they cannot use any magic at all.**

(Although Berserk halves the Charge Time of your character, it does not affect the actual Animation Time of attacking, so it ends up being around 2x, instead of the theoretical 3x.)

**Berserked Physical DPSs will generally do more damage than Magical DPSs (against single targets), so it's worth having at least 1 in your party.**

## Dumb Healing Gambits

This is a specific issue when running multiple healers, especially 3 of them.

When 1 character is damaged, the gambit system is pretty smart, and they will correctly use the amount of healing necessary to fully heal them, and no more.

However, when multiple characters are damaged, the gambit system system becomes dumb and will try heal individually heal every character, forgetting that the most useful healing spells are AOE.

**It's for this reason that I dislike running 3 healers, so if the 3rd character isn't healing, there's no opportunity cost for that 3rd character to be permanently berserked as a Physical DPS anyways.**

When I run 2 healers, I put them at different thresholds, so if multiple characters are slightly damaged, only 1 of them will try to heal.

## Conclusion

For the previous reasons, Armor and Berserk (and Dumb Healing Gambits to a degree), rather than building 3 do-everythings, it's generally more effective for characters to specialize as a Physical DPS or a Spellcaster.

A team consisting of 2 Spellcasters, with at least 1 of them capable of healing and the other capable of damaging spells, with 1 Berserked Physical DPS is a very solid, well balanced team.

Of course, for specific scenarios, you might run 3 Spellcasters, or you might run 1 Spellcaster (as a healer) and 2 Berserked Physical DPSs.

With this out of the way, we can cover how to build characters as either a Physical DPS or a Spellcaster.

# Building Physical DPS

**Physical DPSs hit things. Since the most important aspect of a physical DPS is whether or not you have their weapon, it's more important that you have a good weapon for them to use, and less important overall on how you build them.**

Nevertheless, are are a few general optimizations that you can do:

## Pairing Fast Physical Classes With Slow Physical Classes

**If you pair against this table, you'll almost always make a good Physical DPS, no matter what stage of the game you are in** (as long as you actually get their weapons).

| **"Slow" Classes**                | **"Fast" Classes**                |
|-----------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Uhlan                             | Archer                            |
| Knight                            | Shikari                           |
| Monk                              | Bushi                             |
| Foebreaker*                       | Machinist                         |
|                                   | Time Battlemage                   |

*If you care about endgame viability, (and won't just respec your way out of it), because Foebreaker's endgame weapon falls off, the only "Fast" class that should pair them with is Bushi, since Bushi's the only in the "Fast" category with good endgame weapons.

The rest of this section is used to justify this and can be skipped if you do not care about the reasons.

### Swiftness

In Final Fantasy XII, there are passives that increase your character's Speed, called Swiftness. Each one increases your speed by *roughly* 10%.

There are a maximum of 3 levels of Swiftness. We can divide the physical classes based on their Swiftness:

| **Swiftness 1 or 2**              | **Swiftness 3**                   |
|-----------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Uhlan                             | Archer                            |
| Knight                            | Shikari                           |
| Foebreaker*                       | Bushi                             |
| Monk*                             | Time Battlemage                   |
|                                   | Machinist                         |

*Foebreaker and Monk can both get Swiftness 3, but you get them very late in the game, so for the purposes of this division, they're considered to have Swiftness 1 or 2.

### Strength

In Final Fantasy XII, almost all physical weapons primarily use the *Strength* stat to compute their damage. Somewhat obviously then, if we want to maximize damage, we also want to maximize *Strength*.

Classes have a varying amount of strength from Battle Lores, but interestingly, you can also see the same division, but from another viewpoint: classes that want more of the stat, *Strength,* and classes that naturally have a lot of *Strength.*

| **High Strength**                 | **Low Strength**                  |
|-----------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Knight                            | Archer                            |
| Uhlan                             | Shikari                           |
| Foebreaker                        | Bushi                             |
| Monk                              | Time Battlemage*                  |
|                                   | Machinist**                       |

The main reason why classes have high *Strength* is mostly because they use Heavy Armor, which directly increases your *Strength* stat when worn. The exception is Monk, who just has so much built in *Strength* because they have 16 Battle Lores, the most of any class, and each Battle Lore increases your Strength by 1.

The main reason why the classes have low *Strength* is mostly because they do **not** have Heavy Armor.

*Time Battlemage is an exception. They wear Heavy Armor, but because their weapon is so weak anyways that they would want a better weapon from one of the stronger classes.

**Machinist does not use the *Strength* stat when computing damage, but nevertheless, they can benefit from being paired with a high *Strength* character, although the reason for this is a little more of an intermediate level.

### Rounding Out Weapon Progression

Let's recall the table about early vs. late game physical classes based around their weapon availability.

| **Early to Mid Game**             | **Mid to Late Game**              |
|-----------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Foebreaker                        | Uhlan                             |
| Archer                            | Bushi                             |
| Shikari                           | Monk                              |
| Machinist                         | Knight                            |

If you notice, this is remarkably similar to the previous tables, the main difference being that Foebreaker and Bushi have swapped places! (Also, Time Battlemage is excluded because they have bad weapons in general.)

### Summary

In this sense, this table shows classes that are "Fast" to reach their maximum power vs. classes that are "Slow" to reach their maximum power, which is identical to the one at the top.

| **"Slow" Classes**                | **"Fast" Classes**                |
|-----------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Uhlan                             | Archer                            |
| Knight                            | Shikari                           |
| Monk                              | Bushi                             |
| Foebreaker*                       | Machinist                         |
|                                   | Time Battlemage                   |

*If you care about endgame viability, (and won't just respec your way out of it), because Foebreaker's endgame weapon falls off, the only "Fast" class that should pair them with is Bushi, since Bushi's the only in the "Fast" category with good endgame weapons.

## Pairing Knight, Shikari, and Machinist with a Mystic Armor Class

Knight has *Excalibur,* a weapon that deals Holy damage. Shikari has *Yagyu Darkblade,* a weapon that deals Dark damage. Machinist has *Dark Ammo,* which makes their gun deal *Dark* damage.

If you remember from the section on Elemental Boosting, you might have noticed that *White Robes* and *Black Robes* boost Holy and Dark respectively.

As these are Mystic Armor, you can boost the damage of these weapons by 50% if you pair either Knight, Shikari, or Machinist with a Mystic Armor class (White Mage, Black Mage, Red Battlemage, Bushi).

It's also worth mentioning that in the endgame, elemental damage is often resisted (and Dark is especially resisted), so this isn't the most applicable. Still, in the endgame, this can be very useful for a couple of fights.

(Uhlan also has a Holy weapon, *Holy Lance,* but it isn't nearly as powerful as any of the other weapons, so it isn't as important.)

## Physical DPS Gambits

This is really, really simple:

1. Self: *Berserk* or *Bacchus's Wine*
2. Party Leader's Target: Attack
3. Lowest HP Enemy: Attack

They may not even use gambits if they're wearing *Berserker Braces,* an accessory that causes the character to always be berserked.

# Building Spellcasters

**In general, most Spellcasters (especially Black Mage and White Mage) are very strong, so pairing them with any class will more or less result in a good combination.** (Time Battlemage, on the other hand, is not very strong, and so requires special consideration.)

**Almost any combination of classes, even two Magic classes, will have plenty of HP, so it's not a big deal to specifically optimize for "bulk."** (The only exception is White Mage/Time Battlemage, and that's part of the reason why I do not suggest that combination.)

Because magic damage and healing are governed by the same stat, MAG, it's very, very good to have your Spellcasters capable of both magic damage and healing. This is because magic damage will, even with ultimate weapons, outdamage physical weapons with a high enough stat differential (because you're wearing Mystic Armor).

The Spellcasters in this game differ greatly for what they're missing, so we will cover each of them separately.

## Black Mage

This class is already complete as a magical DPS, as they are the primary magical DPS in the game. They're also excellent at inflicting enemies with status effects.

**The main thing they're missing is healing and buffs**, which is solved by pairing one of these classes with them:

| **Early and Mid Game**              | **Endgame**                         |
|-------------------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| White Mage                          |                                     |
| Red Battlemage                      | Knight*                             |
| Time Battlemage*                    | Monk*                               |

*These require Espers to some degree.

White Mage and Knight have the added benefit of Faith, causing Black Mage's spells to hit harder.

## White Mage

This class is already complete as a healer, being the primary healer of the game. They're also excellent at support, gaining some amazing buffs.

**The main thing that it's missing is damage**, which can be solved by sticking on **any physical DPS class** (with Shikari being a standout option) **or Black Mage** if you want to deal magical DPS instead.

**Dealing damage as White Mage makes White Mage better at healing, because dealing damage *restores* MP**, so White Mage will have a lot more MP to work with if they also deal damage when not healing.

When filling time as a healer, Black Mage will do more damage than any other physical DPS class and regenerate MP better when wearing Mystic Armor and boosted elemental spells, even compared with ultimate weapons (until you're able to have both 99 STR and 99 MAG).

## Red Battlemage

As a healer, they can do everything that White Mage can do in the early and mid game, but better, as they also have access to some damaging spells.

In the late game, as a healer, White Mage is just better due to better healing spells and buffs.

As a magical DPS, Black Mage throughout the entire game due to non-elemental spells and easier access to elemental boosting equipment.

**This makes them a sort of awkward class to pair, as in the early and mid game, they're basically already complete, and in the endgame, there's no other class that can solve their endgame awkwardness.**

Here's 4 class combinations that are good for Red Battlemage, with the first 2 being the most common:

### Shikari
- This doubles down on Red Battlemage's effectiveness in the early and mid game by giving them even more survivability, but this is more of an advanced player topic.
- In the endgame, Shikari pretty much entirely takes over as a Physical DPS with either boosted Yagyu Darkblade or Heavy Armor (via Red Battlemage esper).

### Archer
- This gives Red Battlemage access to boosted Fira/Firaga/Ardor, but this only puts them on par with Black Mage. (Ardor, the "ultimate" Fire-element spell that is exclusive to Red Battlemage has similar DPS to Firaga. Although Ardor is more powerful per cast, it also has a longer animation. The two mostly cancel out, so they deal similar DPS.)
- Archer will out damage Red Battlemage by itself as a Berserked Physical DPS, which is also awkward.
- You're generally better off picking Black Mage/White Mage than this (and sticking Archer on a Heavy Armor class), but this is a valid option if you're running 12 jobs.

### Uhlan
- Since Uhlan is basically already complete as a Physical DPS (only missing Swiftness 3, all other endgame Physical classes want something), you can make Red Battlemage into a Physical DPS in the endgame.
- This is also probably one of the only combinations in which you'll actually see Boosted Holy Lance if you're not respecing.

### Knight/Monk
- In the early to mid game, neither of these classes contributes much to Red Battlemage.
- In the endgame, these can fix the White Magic issues of Red Battlemage, but Red Battlemage really only contributes Mystic Armor to those classes, so in the endgame, both of those classes would rather have another class.

You can also just respec Red Battlemage away in the endgame instead of trying to make them good.

## Time Battlemage

Time Battlemage can be an incredible support unit, but the lack of damage and the lack of endgame healing makes Time Battlemage incredibly awkward.

You can fix both by sticking Monk or Knight onto Time Battlemage, with Monk being preferred, as Knight would like Mystic Armor (for boosting *Excalibur* and healing), and Monk having much more White Magic than Knight.

You can also use Black Mage to fix their damage, but that won't fix their endgame healing issues.

## Monk

They have endgame healing and physical DPS, but they don't have early game healing. In addition, their weapon type is very STR reliant, but they do not have Heavy Armor. This makes Time Battlemage the standout choice, because of early game healing, Time Battlemage's buffs, and Heavy Armor.

This is also one of the few armor class specialization exceptions, as Monk/Time Battlemage likes to heal, but doesn't have Mystic Armor. They will heal for a lot less as compared to Mystic Armor healers, and it can be troublesome if they're your only healer. Monk eventually gets Renew though, a MAG ignoring healing spell, so it's not a big deal.

## Knight

They have endgame healing and physical DPS, but they don't have early game healing. However, unlike Monk, which takes Time Battlemage due in large part to gaining Heavy Armor and Cura, Knight would like Mystic Armor because boosted Excalibur is good (in the very endgame) and they lack Renew, a MAG ignoring healing spell.

This makes Knight much trickier to build for comprehensive power, but I'll go over each Mystic Armor option:

### Red Battlemage
- Red Battlemage/Knight is a very comprehensively powerful option, with strong healing, buffs, and physical DPS throughout the game.
- However, this combination only has Swiftness 1. Each rank of Swiftness is worth 10% Physical DPS when non-berserked and 5% Physical DPS when berserked. This is a little gross because they're reliant on Physical DPS in the endgame. (Because Berserk halves the charge time, it also halves DPS increase of both Swiftness and Haste.)
- You can always respec Red Battlemage away in the endgame!

### Black Mage
- Similar to Red Battlemage/Knight, but it trades in some early game healing for better magical damage throughout the entire game. This also provides an incredibly strong magical DPS option when using Mystic Armor and Shields. (Remember that when wearing Mystic Armor, magic will do more damage than even endgame weapons if the stat difference is at least 24, aka, wearing all Mystic Armor.)
- This combination has Swiftness 2, which isn't nearly as gross as Red Battlemage/Knight, as they're not reliant on Physical DPS in the endgame while non-berserked.

### Bushi
- The difference between this and Black Mage is that you trade in all of the Black Magic for Swiftness 3 in the endgame. Since they're significantly less powerful as a non-berserked DPS now (due to missing Black Magic for the entire game), their main advantage is that when Berserked, they have 5% more DPS. I don't think this is worth it if you only run 1 Knight (due to 12 jobs), but it's worth running if you run 2 Knights.

## Spellcaster Gambits

Spellcasters will not have everything in this list. This is more to show a general gambit list to be taken from.

1. Ally: HP < X%: Strongest Healing Spell
2. Ally: Any: Esuna
3. Ally: Any: Raise/Arise
4. DPS: Appropriate damage buffs (*Bravery, Faith, Berserk, Haste/ga*, etc)
5. Ally: Any: Defensive buffs (*Protect/ga, Shell/ga*)
6. Any Enemy: HP = 100%: Steal
7. Spells that Exploit enemy weaknesses (this is optional, boosted spells often one-shot anyways)
8. Attack or Strongest Magic Spell
9. Self: Quality of Life buffs (*Libra*, *Float*)
10. Any Ally: Cura (This is to top off any party member that took damage after a fight.)

If you run multiple healers, you should decide which one should be your primary healer and secondary healer.

### Main Healer or Secondary Healer?

If you have multiple Spellcasters capable of healing in your party, then you'll have to decide which character should be the main healer and which one should be the secondary healer.

I like to have my main healer heal at 70% and my secondary healer heal at 40%.
