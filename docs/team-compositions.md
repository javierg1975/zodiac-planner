## Beginner Team

If all you want is a basic team combination, here is a recommended setup. As previously stated, *Final Fantasy XII: The Zodiac Age* is easy enough to be completed with any combination of 12 classes, so instead of optimizing for absolute power over the entire game, these class combinations are the easiest to use and most flexible while also making sure they have the important endgame considerations.

This can definitely be further optimized. However, all further optimizations come at the cost of increased complexity (like class "timings" or specific playstyles), so further optimization is an exercise to the reader.

### Magic Users

- **Vaan**: Red Battlemage/Knight
- **Penelo**: White Mage/Shikari
- **Fran**: Monk/Time Battlemage
- **Ashe**: Black Mage/White Mage

### Physical DPS

- **Balthier**: Foebreaker/Bushi
- **Basch**: Archer/Uhlan

### How to Use

When you only have access to 1 class, pick the first listed class for each character. As for Esper Allocation, follow the Esper Priority Table to pick espers for these combinations.

The **Physical DPSs** are intended to constantly be attacking with Physical weapons under the influence of *Berserk*, the largest Physical DPS increase (other than 4x Speed).

The **Magical Users** are obviously intended to use magic (which usually means healing magic and buffs). When they're not healing, they're intended to deal damage, either with their weapons or with offensive magic. They are not usually intended to be under the influence of *Berserk*, as *Berserk* prevents you from casting magic. However, for the combinations that have good weapons, you can *Berserk* them and treat them as if they were a Physical DPS instead.

### Building Your Teams

For general use, pick any **2 Magic Users and 1 Physical DPS**. Following that, any combination is fine. There are differences between the Magic Users, but as they can all properly heal throughout the entire game, which ones you use are based on your needs.

For example, here are some considerations for team building. Jobs in **bold** will be the reason why you bring them along:

- **If you want more Magical DPS**, bring any of the following:
  - **Red Battlemage**/Knight
  - **Black Mage**/White Mage

- **If you want more Physical DPS**, bring any of the following:
  - White Mage/**Shikari**
  - **Monk**/Time Battlemage
  - Red Battlemage/**Knight**

- **If you want more healing**, bring any of the following:
  - **White Mage**/Shikari
  - Black Mage/**White Mage**

- **If you want more buffs**, bring along:
  - Monk/**Time Battlemage**

It doesn't exactly matter which ones you bring, as they all are good in their own ways. Experiment and find out what you prefer.

### Team Rotation Strategy

Since there are a total of 4 Magic Users and 2 Physical DPSs, you can split them into 2 teams, each composed of 2 Magic Users and 1 Physical DPS. This ensures everyone is evenly leveled (because characters that are not in the active party do not receive EXP). Other people like to have a team specifically for story content and another team for optional content to balance out the game's difficulty.

Note that having 2 Magic Users and 1 Physical DPS is not required - you can run 3 Magic Users at once or 1 Magic User and 2 Physical DPSs if you would like. You generally want at least 1 Magic User though, as playing the game without a healer is somewhat difficult. 2 Magic Users and 1 Physical DPS is just the easiest to use team throughout the game.

### On Skipping Machinist/Using All 12 Jobs

If you noticed, we skipped Machinist in these combinations. This is because Machinist is not a class that was meant for beginners to use. Unless you get their ultimate weapon early (or are playing on weak mode), Machinist is a class that will always be underpowered compared to other classes.

Obtaining their ultimate weapon early is out of reach for beginners because of the knowledge required and difficulty involved. Or in other words, if you can get their weapon early, you're not a beginner anymore. And originally, weak mode was only offered to players that have already beaten the game once.

Even so, if you are a beginner and still want to use Machinist (without getting their ultimate weapon early), you can replace Black Mage/White Mage with Black Mage/Machinist. You will have to be a little bit more careful when building picking your 2 Magic Users, as Black Mage/Machinist does not have any healing, but other than that, everything else is the same.

If you are an advanced player and want to get their ultimate weapon early, you should build your own team combination, as this team composition is meant for beginners, and is not optimized at all around Machinist's potential power.

## Understanding Team Building

### Why Evasion Matters

It's a pretty common misconception that evasion is worthless because hunts and bosses ignore it. Although this is partly true, most hunts and bosses ignore physical evasion, this ignores that **normal enemies almost never ignore evasion.**

**The reason why you'd want evasion against normal enemies is because if you go to endgame areas early, evasion will greatly decrease the amount of damage that you take, allowing you to much more easily complete the area and pick up the treasure lying around.**

**Some of these endgame normal enemies have stats that equal, or even exceed, the stats of hunts that you have access to at the same time.**

**It's also important to note that if your characters take less damage, your characters will need less healing, so ultimately, they have more time to deal damage instead. This is part of the reason why survivability can *increase* your DPS.**

**Additionally, if you're trying to distribute all damage onto 1 character, it's obviously helpful if they can actually survive that damage, and evasion can help with that.**

Of course, in the endgame, there's no more optional areas to do early, and you have access to so many resources, in terms of gear, levels, consumable items, so evasion becomes less important in the endgame, as you can brute force it with enough time spent in the game.

But even in the endgame, there are uses for evasion, the primary one being the Abysteel junction in Henne Mines part 2, the best spot for level grinding.

## The Leader Role

With the background information out of the way, we can now define the Leader role.

The Leader Role isn't so much of a specific role, so much as it's a plan on where the damage should go, so the other party members can maintain HP% augments.

Because of this, their only requirement is that they take the team's damage, not die, and support the team as much as they can without the expectation of maintaining HP% augments.

Generally, you want to lead the party with them, as enemies will often attack the first enemy that they see. For this reason, it's best to avoid Berserked Physical DPSs, as you cannot directly control them.

Since their main role is to not die and support the team as much as possible, it's very, very natural to make Healers the Leader, and have the other two party members double down on damage, whether magical or physical, although strictly speaking, this isn't something that you have to do.

With this in mind, here are the best combinations for the Leader role.

### Red Battlemage/Shikari (Early to Mid Game)

Due to the focus on evasion early on, the best class in this regard is Shikari, as they have access to *Main Gauche*, a dagger that does somewhat poor damage, but has 50% evasion on it, and you can still wear a shield with it! You can buy this weapon in *Jahara* (~35% of the game), so it's easy to get as well.

The best class to pair with Shikari while using *Main Gauche* is Red Battlemage because they're the only single class that can heal and deal magical damage (so they can ignore the poor attack rating on *Main Gauche*).

(Black Mage will not get to use their elemental boosting staves while using *Main Gauche*, so Red Battlemage doesn't lose much DPS as compared to Black Mage, until Black Mage acquires non-elemental spells.)

#### On Shikari/White Mage

This is also good, and is a better option than Red Battlemage/Shikari in the endgame. Despite this, Shikari/White Mage is worse than Black Mage/Knight or Monk/Time Battlemage in the endgame, so it's kind of a wash.

Also, *Main Gauche* doesn't do very much damage, White Mage/Shikari will tend to have MP issues while using *Main Gauche*. And in terms of a damage dealer, Shikari will do less DPS than a spell slinging Red Battlemage in the early and mid game, mostly due to wearing Mystic Armor for healing. In the endgame, as a pure Physical DPS, they will also do less DPS, due to the lack of Heavy Armor. (Red Battlemage has access to endgame Heavy Armor via an Esper.)

### Knight/Black Mage (Endgame Only)

Knight/Black Mage is more or less the endgame version of Shikari/Red Battlemage.

In the endgame, it's not so important that you use *Main Gauche* specifically, as you gain access to good shields with 50 or more evasion. From a theoretical perspective, not *as* tanky, but still really tanky.

However, unlike Red Battlemage/Shikari, they will eventually have access to endgame White Magic, including Bravery and Faith, allowing them to take over as the evasion tank, as they keep up with endgame healing and buffs.

They will also have non-elemental spells, incredibly useful to use as you cannot use the elemental boosting staves (or *Burning Bow*) with shields, as they are all two-handed, and being a Black Mage and all, means that they can serve as one of the best Magical DPSs in the game.

In addition, they also serve as one of the best Physical DPSs in the game, due to Knight being a good Physical DPS class and having access to Mystic Armor (for Holy Boosted Excalibur.)

#### On Knight/Bushi

Bushi has 3 Swiftness Lores, while Black Mage has 2. This is a 5% Physical DPS difference while berserked, while Knight/Black Mage is so much more versatile when non-berserked, especially before getting Tier 2 or higher weapons.

### Monk/Time Battlemage (Mid to Endgame)

This combination functions a little bit differently than the other two combinations as a leader.

Although they have massive amounts of HP, due to their lesser amount of evasion and worse healing, they generally aren't as durable as the other two combinations (though still plenty durable if you play the game normally).

The lack of Mystic Armor and Faith means that their healing will not be as effective as the two characters for most of the game, although in the endgame, this is more of a "theoretical" limit, rather than a practical one, as Curaja restores a lot of health, and they will eventually get the spell *Renew*, which just restores 100% of HP, regardless of MAG or Faith.

They also won't reach the damage heights of Red Battlemage/Shikari in the early game and Knight/Black Mage in the late game, although they are still incredibly solid as a damage dealer throughout the game.

What they have instead is the best Physical DPS utility, as they're the only class combination with Haste, Bravery, Expose (which reduces enemy defense), Renew, and Reverse (the last two are used to set allied HP to 1).

What this means is that in terms of maximizing Physical DPS *as a team*, no other combination comes close.

#### On Genji Gloves on Monk

You may be wondering if Genji Gloves (so Knight/Foebreaker/Bushi) worth it on Monk over Time Battlemage. The short answer is no, it's not.

It's worth noting for Monk that Haste contributes about 20% DPS for non-berserked Physical DPSs, while Genji Gloves provides 25% DPS on Kanya *only*, so a non-berserked, hasted Monk/Time Battlemage will have similar individual DPS to a non-berserked, non-hasted, Genji Gloves Monk.

By providing Haste to the entire team, however, they can easily make up that missing 5%, so this combination increases your overall DPS more than Monk/Genji Gloves while non-berserked.

Of course, before you have 99 STR with Genji Armor, Monk/Time Battlemage will deal far more damage due to having access to better Heavy Armor, and they have significantly better support throughout the entire game to boot.

As a Genji Gloves Berserked DPS, Bushi and Knight are both generally better options than Monk and Monk doesn't really contribute anything particularly special to Bushi that Knight or Uhlan doesn't.

### Leader Gambits

1. Ally: HP < X%: Strongest Healing Spell (<40% for secondary Healer, <70% for main Healer)
2. Ally: Any: Esuna
3. Ally: Any: Raise/Arise
4. **Self: Decoy**
5. DPS: Appropriate damage buffs (*Bravery, Faith, Berserk, Haste/ga*, etc)
6. Ally: Any: Defensive buffs (*Protect/ga, Shell/ga*)
7. Any Enemy: HP = 100%: Steal
8. Spells that Exploit enemy weaknesses (this is the very optional, in case you run out of gambits)
9. Attack or Strongest Magic Spell
10. Self: Quality of Life buffs (*Libra*, *Float*)
11. Any Ally: Cura (This is to top off any party member that took damage during a fight to trigger Focus/Serenity.)

The main gambit difference between this and a Spellcaster is Self: Decoy somewhere in the gambits.

#### Main Healer or Secondary Healer: Part 2

Another thing that you should now consider is that if your Leader is healing is whether they'll be the main healer or the secondary healer.

If the Leader is the main healer, they will not heal as much (due to HP% augments), but your secondary healer will be able to do more damage.

If the Leader is the secondary healer, the secondary will not deal as much damage (because they're healing instead), but your team will have more healing.

In easy areas, I would lean towards making the Leader the main healer for higher DPS, and in difficult areas, I would lean towards making the Leader the secondary leader for better healing.

## Intermediate Team Combinations

You can just put all three of these leaders into your party because there isn't actually any big opportunity cost to doing so. They're all already almost optimal as DPSs go anyways, so you can just take all three of them:

- **Red Battlemage/Shikari**
- **Knight/Black Mage**
- **Monk/Time Battlemage**

The final three characters can be whatever, really.

Characters that would be nice on top, for versatility:

- Another Healer (White Mage)
- Another Elemental Boosted Magical DPS (Black Mage)
- Physical DPSs to fill the rest of the characters.

**White Mage/Black Mage** conveniently covers the first two.

**Bushi/Knight** is another Physical DPS. Knight and Bushi both have really good weapons. This Knight, however, isn't going to bother with any magic spells from Espers and is just going to be permanently berserked.

**Archer/Uhlan** is also another Physical DPS. They're going to be permanently Berserked, so anything past that doesn't matter. I like taking Archer for their good item usage in emergencies and good weapon progression, transitioning to Zodiac Spear in the endgame.

If you're running 12 jobs for some reason, I'd run White Mage/Machinist, Bushi/Foebreaker, and Uhlan/Archer instead, but any variation of these classes will be fine.

## Advanced Job Utility

In the Basic section, we ignored a lot of the extra utility that the jobs can provide, especially the Physical ones.

This section will cover this utility. Although it's not usually very important, I still wanted to at least mention it.

### Archer

They have access to 3 Phoenix Lores, which means that their Phoenix Downs heal for 50% Max HP. When combined with *Pheasant Netsuke* (after *Raithwall, ~35%*), an accessory that doubles the potency of items, Phoenix Downs will restore 100% life.

Since all items have 0 CT, it means that you can revive characters to 100% before the enemy has the chance to act again if you set the battle speed to its minimum (this is a setting in the options and is separate from the entire game speed multiplier).

This actually makes Archer the strongest healer per CT, even stronger than White Mage for the entire game, as long as you're willing to spam Phoenix Downs, especially if you try to take on a fight that's out of your league.

From my experience, no boss can actually outdamage Pheasant Netsuke + Phoenix Down spam and it's easy enough to have 99 of them.

They also have Cura, but it's fairly irrelevant for most builds, as they are usually permanently berserked (so they can't cast spells). Even if they weren't berserked, they don't have a very good MAG stat unless they were paired with Bushi or something, so it's not really worth building around, especially when Time Battlemage and Red Battlemage also provide Cura but significantly more utility as a spellcaster.

### Black Mage

Status effects are actually useful in Final Fantasy XII. A lot of normal enemies and even hunts can be inflicted with incredibly debilitating status effects. Black Mage is excellent at inflicting status effects, especially with *Indigo Pendant* (~60% at the earliest), which basically ensures that all status effects have a 100% chance to be inflicted.

### Shikari

They have *Main Gauche*, which provides +50% evasion. Very useful. They also have access to a good amount of guns for some reason.

### Red Battlemage

It's sort of an exaggeration to say they cannot heal effectively in the endgame. 7 out of the 12 classes have access to either *Demon Shield* or *Black Mask*, both of which allow the wearer to absorb Dark damage. This puts their healing on par with Curaja if you use this strategy.

However, this comes at a significant DPS penalty for most characters (due to having to wear Mystic Armor or a Shield).

This still doesn't make up for the fact that they're missing endgame buffs.

Their Time Magic utility will be covered under Time Battlemage.

### Time Battlemage

Again, they have access to status effects, although more niche ones as compared to Black Mage, but they still can be useful.

**Reflect** can be useful for maximizing Magical DPS. You put reflect onto all of your characters and cast AOE spells on yourself. This essentially multi-casts the spell! This is kind of a hassle though because it makes using healing spells difficult (they will also be reflected), but it can be incredibly strong if you're willing to pull it out.

**Balance** is a spell that deals damage based off of the difference between your current HP and your max HP. If you use Infuse with it (from another character), you can reduce your HP to 10 fairly easily. Because you can get this before Raithwall, this can be an incredibly damaging spell throughout the game, but it's also kind of a hassle to set up.

(Time Battlemage has both Reflect and Balance, but not the next two spells.)

**Warp** can instantly kill most normal enemies in an AOE (with 100% chance too if you use *Indigo Pendant*), but you receive no EXP or LP.

**Float** is a really, really nice quality of life spell, allowing you to ignore traps. It's kind of difficult to get your party members to avoid traps and there are some locations (like the entrances to the Necrohol of Nabudis) where they're unavoidable.

Stepping on a trap won't cause a game over, but they can be incredibly annoying, like ones that inflict Disease on your characters or the ones that reduce your MP to 0. In terms of quality of life, this is actually more important than Haste.

### Machinist

They have measures, which are fun little ways to gain buffs without spending MP. Unfortunately, the cost is that you have to manually menu to gain their buffs. However, they're 1 handed and give 35 evasion, which is quite good. Worse than Shikari, but still pretty good.

They can also get Hastega really late in the game via Esper. Hastega takes a lot of MP though, and you get this really, really late.

### Foebreaker

They have access to all 4 stat breaking techniques. Every boss is vulnerable to them and you can use them to reduce an enemy's damage and defense to 0, allowing you trivally beat the fight, since they'll do 0 damage.

You get the damage reducing ones late in the game though.

Unfortunately, other classes also have them, especially Monk/Time Battlemage.

Their endgame weapon can compete with the other best weapons (like Tournesol, Zodiac Spear, and Kumbha) in DPS if you reduce the enemy's defense to 0. They're very defense sensitive.

### Bushi

Shades of Black can be useful in the midgame for hitting flying enemies, but a Berserked Physical DPS is usually better.

## Summary: Building Your Optimal Team

### Core Principles

1. **Have at least one healer** - Makes the game significantly easier
2. **Balance magic and physical damage** - 2 Magic Users + 1 Physical DPS is the most flexible
3. **Understand the Leader role** - One character absorbs damage while others maintain HP% augments
4. **Consider game phase** - Some combinations shine early, others late

### Recommended Core Team

For most playthroughs, running all three leader combinations works excellently:

1. **Red Battlemage/Shikari** - Early to mid game tank/healer
2. **Knight/Black Mage** - Endgame tank/healer with magical and physical prowess
3. **Monk/Time Battlemage** - Team DPS support with buffs and utility

Then fill the remaining slots with:
- **White Mage/Black Mage** - Versatile healer with magical DPS
- **Bushi/Knight** - Berserked physical DPS powerhouse
- **Archer/Uhlan** - Berserked physical DPS with item utility

This gives you maximum flexibility to tackle any content in the game while maintaining strong synergy and role coverage.
