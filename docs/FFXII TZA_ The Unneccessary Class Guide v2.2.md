# Background/Context

## *Final Fantasy XII: The Zodiac Age* does not need optimization

About a year after Final Fantasy XII originally released, there was an
"international" version of Final Fantasy XII released called
*International Zodiac Job* *System.* **In the original version of the
job system, you could only pick 1 class on each character.**

**Despite characters being much more limited in ability in
*International Zodiac Job System,* it was generally considered to be
easier compared to the original game, the biggest ones being:**

- **Damage/healing cap was removed**

  - This means that although characters were more limited in the
    *breadth* of what they could do, each character's *maximum* strength
    was uncapped.

- **A 2x speed multiplier was introduced**

  - As your characters' strength in Final Fantasy XII heavily depends on
    your gear and levels, grinding became a lot easier.

- **Stackable, unremovable stat breaks work on every enemy**

  - This means you can reduce the damage that bosses deal to you to
    virtually 0.

- **Your HP in the early game is greatly increased, due to generally
  better HP augments**

  - In the early game of FFXII, your HP would start at 100 and top out
    around 1000, but in the Zodiac Age, it's not difficult to get an
    extra 1000 or 2000 HP, which would greatly increase the amount of HP
    that you would have.

- **Invisible "cheat" equipment were added**

  - The stats on these are so good, it's basically cheating to use them.

  - The downside of them is that obtaining them is normally, fairly time
    consuming, so they were the developers' way of ensuring that given
    enough time, literally any combination of classes can complete all
    content.

There are other adjustments as well, but these are the primary reasons.
The original *Final Fantasy XII*, nor its re-release, *International
Zodiac Job* *System* were never considered to be particularly difficult
games, nor did they require minmaxing to complete. You can beat
*International Zodiac Job System* with 6 White Mages, if you want.

I mention this because ***Final Fantasy XII: The Zodiac Age* is, in its
current form, even easier because:**

- **You can pick 2 classes per character**

  - Each character can do even more as compared to *International Zodiac
    Job System.*

- **You can respec your character classes now**

  - Classes in *The Zodiac Age* have different windows in which they're
    effective, mostly due to gear and spell availability.

  - You're also not locked into class decisions that you make, although
    as I said before, *International Zodiac Job System* was never
    considered to be particularly difficult with permanent classes.

- **You can keep equipment and items from Trial Mode now**

  - If you choose to, Trial Mode gives you access to equipment far
    earlier in the game than usual, and can give you some pretty
    overpowered stuff early.

- **4x speed was added**

  - 4x speed is like, twice as fast as 2x speed.

- **Auto Save**

  - Not just a quality of life feature, this also greatly speeds up
    farming of difficult drops from difficult to spawn enemies.

**So, if you just want to play the game, and pick whatever classes, just
go for it. It's completely unnecessary to optimize *Final Fantasy XII:
The Zodiac Age.***

**Even so, this guide is intended for players who want to optimize this
game at a higher level.**

By the way, this guide's conclusions are based on the information under
the section, [[Theoretical
Information]{.underline}](#theoretical-information). If I make a
comparative claim about DPS, it *should* be justified under one of the
tables in there.

# Recommended Beginner Team

If all you want is a basic team combination, here is my own
recommendation. As previously stated, *Final Fantasy XII: The Zodiac
Age* is easy enough to be completed with any combination of 12 classes,
so instead of optimizing for absolute power over the entire game, these
class combinations are the easiest to use and most flexible while also
making sure they have the important endgame considerations.

This can definitely be further optimized. However, all further
optimizations come at the cost of increased complexity (like class
"timings" or specific playstyles), so further optimization is an
exercise to the reader.

## Magic Users

Vaan: Red Battlemage/Knight

Penelo: White Mage/Shikari

Fran: Monk/Time Battlemage

Ashe: Black Mage/White Mage

## Physical DPS

Balthier: Foebreaker/Bushi

Basch: Archer/Uhlan

## How to use

When you only have access to 1 class, pick the first listed class for
each character. As for Esper Allocation, follow the [[Esper Priority
Table]{.underline}](#esper-priority-table) to pick espers for these
combinations.

The Physical DPSs are intended to constantly be attacking with Physical
weapons under the influence of *Berserk,* the largest Physical DPS
increase (other than 4x Speed).

The Magical Users are obviously intended to use magic (which usually
means healing magic and buffs). When they're not healing, they're
intended to deal damage, either with their weapons or with offensive
magic. They are not usually intended to be under the influence of
*Berserk,* as *Berserk* prevents you from casting magic. However, for
the combinations that have good weapons, you can *Berserk* them and
treat them as if they were a Physical DPS instead.

As for the teams themselves, you'll need to do that yourself.
Thankfully, this is incredibly easy with these combinations.

For general use, pick any 2 Magic Users and 1 Physical DPS. Following
that, any combination is fine. There are differences between the Magic
Users, but as they can all properly heal throughout the entire game,
which ones you use are based on your needs.

For example, here are some considerations for team building. Jobs in
bold will be the reason why you bring them along:

- If you want more Magical DPS, bring any of the following:

  - **Red Battlemage**/Knight

  - **Black Mage**/White Mage

- If you want more Physical DPS, bring any of the following:

  - White Mage/**Shikari**

  - **Monk**/Time Battlemage

  - Red Battlemage/**Knight**

- If you want more healing, bring any of the following:

  - **White Mage**/Shikari

  - Black Mage/**White Mage**

- If you want more buffs, bring along:

  - Monk/**Time Battlemage**

It doesn't exactly matter which ones you bring, as they all are good in
their own ways. Experiment and find out what you prefer.

Since there are a total of 4 Magic Users and 2 Physical DPSs, you can
split them into 2 teams, each composed of 2 Magic Users and 1 Physical
DPS. I do this to make sure everyone is evenly leveled (because
characters that are not in the active party do not receive EXP). Other
people like to have a team specifically for story content and another
team for optional content to balance out the game's difficulty.

Note that having 2 Magic Users and 1 Physical DPS is not required, you
can run 3 Magic Users at once or 1 Magic User and 2 Physical DPSs if you
would like. You generally want at least 1 Magic User though, as playing
the game without a healer is somewhat difficult. 2 Magic Users and 1
Physical DPS is just the easiest to use team throughout the game.

## On Skipping Machinist/Using All 12 Jobs

If you noticed, we skipped Machinist in these combinations. This is
because Machinist is not a class that was meant for beginners to use.
Unless you get their ultimate weapon early (or are playing on weak
mode), Machinist is a class that will always be underpowered compared to
other classes.

Obtaining their ultimate weapon early is out of reach for beginners
because of the knowledge required and difficulty involved. Or in other
words, if you can get their weapon early, you're not a beginner anymore.
And originally, weak mode was only offered to players that have already
beaten the game once.

Even so, if you are a beginner and still want to use Machinist (without
getting their ultimate weapon early), you can replace Black Mage/White
Mage with Black Mage/Machinist. You will have to be a little bit more
careful when building picking your 2 Magic Users, as Black
Mage/Machinist does not have any healing, but other than that,
everything else is the same.

If you are an advanced player and want to get their ultimate weapon
early, you should build your own team combination, as this team
composition is meant for beginners, and is not optimized at all around
Machinist's potential power.

From here, the actual guide starts.

utility that make them stand out.

## Spellcasters

These are the Spellcasters:

- Black Mage deals great damage and inflicts status effects.

- White Mage heals and has good buffs.

- Red Battlemage can both deal damage and heal, although their damage
  tends to be lower than Black Mage's and Red Battlemages don't usually
  heal very effectively in the late game.

- Time Battlemage has some unique utility that is often very useful and
  early game healing.

- Monk and Knight both get endgame White Magic, so they can be used in
  place of White Mage then.

**Unlike Physical Damage Dealers, all of the magic classes are very good
throughout the game and fairly low effort to use because you can just
buy most spells from the magic shops.**

**Magical DPS falls off at the start of the Great Crystal (\~80%) of the
game, but magical healing is always relevant throughout the game.**

You can get useful late game spells early if you pick them up from
optional dungeons, but even if you don't, mages stay strong throughout
the game, unlike Physical Damage Dealers.

**Because these magic classes are so good on their own, pairing any
other class with them will be a good combination. Even pairing two magic
classes together will be good, although potentially redundant.**

### **On MP**

MP is not an issue in this game, but running out of MP can be annoying.
*Charge* is an ability you get very early on that has a chance to
restore MP, but if you fail, you lose all of your MP. The chance of
success increases the less MP you have.

In practice, this means that *Charge* allows you to have a minimum of
10% to 20% MP at all times, but having to use *Charge* all of the time
can be kind of annoying, as when the gambit AI uses it, they won't move,
falling behind the party.

Somewhat weirdly, because there are passives that give you MP when you
damage enemies and kill enemies, Red Battlemage and especially Black
Mage will often *gain* more MP they use casting damage spells once you
get into the mid game. This makes them incredibly self sustainable, so
they don't need to use *Charge* very often.

As White Mage does not really deal damage as is, it's fairly common for
them to have MP issues. And since damage gives MP, it's incredibly
common to add damage to White Mage solving both their damage and MP
issues.

Black Magic generally gives better MP regeneration than Physical DPS
throughout the game, mostly because MP regeneration is mostly based on
the threshold of damage that you've reached per hit, rather than your
overall DPS. (Look
[[here]{.underline}](https://finalfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Augment_(Final_Fantasy_XII))
for the MP regeneration values based on damage.)

### **On Time Battlemage**

Although I classified Time Battlemage as a Spellcaster, they mostly have

# Character Building

## Specialization over Generalization

In Final Fantasy XII, you generally want to focus your characters as
either Physical DPSs or Spellcasters.

This is mostly down to a few reasons:

### **Armor**

In Final Fantasy XII, there are 3 armor types:

+-----------------------+-----------------------+-----------------------+
| **Light Armor**       | **Heavy Armor**       | **Mystic Armor**      |
+-----------------------+-----------------------+-----------------------+
| Increases HP          | Increases STR         | Increases MAG and MP  |
|                       |                       |                       |
| Balanced DEF and MDEF | Higher DEF than MDEF  | Higher MDEF than DEF  |
+=======================+=======================+=======================+

Heavy Armor and Mystic Armor are very, very important because they
increase the character's damage (and healing) stats by a significant
amount throughout the entire game.

**This is one of the reasons that contributes to specialization in
either physical or magical effectiveness: you get a massive damage and
healing boost if you do so.**

**Until your stats are maxed, level appropriate gear adds on average,
25% *each* to a character's effectiveness in either physical damage or
magical damage/healer.**

**Combined, the difference between wearing stat appropriate gear is
about 50%!**

(For STR weapons with a secondary SPD/MAG/VIT component, the bonus drops
down to 15% on average per piece for Physical DPS, which while not *as*
huge, is still a large contributor towards DPS.)

(You can view the math [[here]{.underline}](#armor-bonuses), if you're
not convinced.)

### **Berserk**

***Berserk* is a status effect that multiplies the damage of physical
attacks by 1.5x and multiples the speed of your character by 2x.
However, in exchange, you will not be able to control the Berserked
character, and that character will only attack random enemies.**

**In practice, this means that the character's Physical DPS will be
multiplied by 2x, but they cannot use any magic at all.**

(Although Berserk halves the Charge Time of your character, it does not
affect the actual Animation Time of attacking, so it ends up being
around 2x, instead of the theoretical 3x.)

**Berserked Physical DPSs will generally do more damage than Magical
DPSs (against single targets), so it's worth having at least 1 in your
party.**

### **Dumb Healing Gambits**

This is a specific issue when running multiple healers, especially 3 of
them.

When 1 character is damaged, the gambit system is pretty smart, and they
will correctly use the amount of healing necessary to fully heal them,
and no more.

However, when multiple characters are damaged, the gambit system system
becomes dumb and will try heal individually heal every character,
forgetting that the most useful healing spells are AOE.

[[Here's]{.underline}](https://youtu.be/smvogiSLfLs) a video showing
what I mean.

**It's for this reason that I dislike running 3 healers, so if the 3rd
character isn't healing, there's no opportunity cost for that 3rd
character to be permanently berserked as a Physical DPS anyways.**

When I run 2 healers, I put them at different thresholds, so if multiple
characters are slightly damaged, only 1 of them will try to heal.

### **Conclusion**

For the previous reasons, Armor and Berserk (and Dumb Healing Gambits to
a degree), rather than building 3 do-everythings, it's generally more
effective for characters to specialize as a Physical DPS or a
Spellcaster.

A team consisting of 2 Spellcasters, with at least 1 of them capable of
healing and the other capable of damaging spells, with 1 Berserked
Physical DPS is a very solid, well balanced team.

Of course, for specific scenarios, you might run 3 Spellcasters, or you
might run 1 Spellcaster (as a healer) and 2 Berserked Physical DPSs.

With this out of the way, we can cover how to build characters as either
a Physical DPS or a Spellcaster.

physical DPS is whether or not you have their weapon, it's more
important that you have a good weapon for them to use, and less
important overall on how you build them.**

### **Pairing Knight, Shikari, and Machinist with a Mystic Armor Class**

Knight has *Excalibur,* a weapon that deals Holy damage. Shikari has
*Yagyu Darkblade,* a weapon that deals Dark damage. Machinist has *Dark
Ammo,* which makes their gun deal *Dark* damage.

If you remember from the section, [[On Elemental
Boosting]{.underline}](#on-elemental-boosting), you might have noticed
that *White Robes* and *Black Robes* boost Holy and Dark respectively.

As these are Mystic Armor, you can boost the damage of these weapons by
50% if you pair either Knight, Shikari, or Machinist with a Mystic Armor
class (White Mage, Black Mage, Red Battlemage, Bushi).

It's also worth mentioning that in the endgame, elemental damage is
often resisted (and Dark is especially resisted), so this isn't the most
applicable. Still, in the endgame, this can be very useful for a couple
of fights.

(Uhlan also has a Holy weapon, *Holy Lance,* but it isn't nearly as
powerful as any of the other weapons, so it isn't as important.)

### **Physical DPS Gambits**

This is really, really simple:

1.  Self: *Berserk* or *Bacchus's Wine*

2.  Party Leader's Target: Attack

3.  Lowest HP Enemy: Attack

They may not even use gambits if they're wearing *Berserker Braces,* an
accessory that causes the character to always be berserked.

## Spellcasters

**In general, most Spellcasters (especially Black Mage and White Mage)
are very strong, so pairing them with any class will more or less result
in a good combination.** (Time Battlemage, on the other hand, is not
very strong, and so requires special consideration.)

**Almost any combination of classes, even two Magic classes, will have
plenty of HP, so it's not a big deal to specifically optimize for
"bulk."** (The only exception is White Mage/Time Battlemage, and that's
part of the reason why I do not suggest that combination.)

Because magic damage and healing are governed by the same stat, MAG,
it's very, very good to have your Spellcasters capable of both magic
damage and healing. This is because magic damage will, even with
ultimate weapons, outdamage physical weapons with a high enough stat
differential (because you're wearing Mystic Armor).

The Spellcasters in this game differ greatly for what they're missing,
so we will cover each of them separately.

### **Black Mage**

This class is already complete as a magical DPS, as they are the primary
magical DPS in the game. They're also excellent at inflicting enemies
with status effects.

**The main thing they're missing is healing and buffs**, which is solved
by pairing one of these classes with them:

+-------------------------------------+-------------------------------------+
| **Early and Mid Game**              | **Endgame**                         |
+-------------------------------------+-------------------------------------+
| White Mage                                                                |
+-------------------------------------+-------------------------------------+
| Red Battlemage                      | Knight\*                            |
|                                     |                                     |
| Time Battlemage\*                   | Monk\*                              |
+=====================================+=====================================+

\*These require Espers to some degree.

White Mage and Knight have the added benefit of Faith, causing Black
Mage's spells to hit harder.

### **White Mage**

This class is already complete as a healer, being the primary healer of
the game. They're also excellent at support, gaining some amazing buffs.

**The main thing that it's missing is damage**, which can be solved by
sticking on **any physical DPS class** (with Shikari being a standout
option) **or Black Mage** if you want to deal magical DPS instead.

**Dealing damage as White Mage makes White Mage better at healing,
because dealing damage *restores* MP**, so White Mage will have a lot
more MP to work with if they also deal damage when not healing.

When filling time as a healer, Black Mage will do more damage than any
other physical DPS class and regenerate MP better when wearing Mystic
Armor and boosted elemental spells, even compared with ultimate weapons
(until you're able to have both 99 STR and 99 MAG).

### **Red Battlemage**

As a healer, they can do everything that White Mage can do in the early
and mid game, but better, as they also have access to some damaging
spells.

In the late game, as a healer, White Mage is just better due to better
healing spells and buffs.

As a magical DPS, Black Mage throughout the entire game due to
non-elemental spells and easier access to elemental boosting equipment.

**This makes them a sort of awkward class to pair, as in the early and
mid game, they're basically already complete, and in the endgame,
there's no other class that can solve their endgame awkwardness.**

Here's 4 class combinations that are good for Red Battlemage, with the
first 2 being the most common:

- Shikari

  - This doubles down on Red Battlemage's effectiveness in the early and
    mid game by giving them even more survivability, but this is more of
    an advanced player topic.

  - In the endgame, Shikari pretty much entirely takes over as a
    Physical DPS with either boosted Yagyu Darkblade or Heavy Armor (via
    Red Battlemage esper).

- Archer

  - This gives Red Battlemage access to boosted Fira/Firaga/Ardor, but
    this only puts them on par with Black Mage. (Ardor, the "ultimate"
    Fire-element spell that is exclusive to Red Battlemage has similar
    DPS to Firaga. Although Ardor is more powerful per cast, it also has
    a longer animation. The two mostly cancel out, so they deal similar
    DPS.)

  - Archer will out damage Red Battlemage by itself as a Berserked
    Physical DPS, which is also awkward.

  - You're generally better off picking Black Mage/White Mage than this
    (and sticking Archer on a Heavy Armor class), but this is a valid
    option if you're running 12 jobs.

- Uhlan

  - Since Uhlan is basically already complete as a Physical DPS (only
    missing Swiftness 3, all other endgame Physical classes want
    something), you can make Red Battlemage into a Physical DPS in the
    endgame.

  - This is also probably one of the only combinations in which you'll
    actually see Boosted Holy Lance if you're not respecing.

- Knight/Monk

  - In the early to mid game, neither of these classes contributes much
    to Red Battlemage.

  - In the endgame, these can fix the White Magic issues of Red
    Battlemage, but Red Battlemage really only contributes Mystic Armor
    to those classes, so in the endgame, both of those classes would
    rather have another class.

You can also just respec Red Battlemage away in the endgame instead of
trying to make them good.

### **Time Battlemage**

Time Battlemage can be an incredible support unit, but the lack of
damage and the lack of endgame healing makes Time Battlemage incredibly
awkward.

You can fix both by sticking Monk or Knight onto Time Battlemage, with
Monk being preferred, as Knight would like Mystic Armor (for boosting
*Excalibur* and healing), and Monk having much more White Magic than
Knight.

You can also use Black Mage to fix their damage, but that won't fix
their endgame healing issues.

### **Monk**

They have endgame healing and physical DPS, but they don't have early
game healing. In addition, their weapon type is very STR reliant, but
they do not have Heavy Armor. This makes Time Battlemage the standout
choice, because of early game healing, Time Battlemage's buffs, and
Heavy Armor.

This is also one of the few armor class specialization exceptions, as
Monk/Time Battlemage likes to heal, but doesn't have Mystic Armor. They
will heal for a lot less as compared to Mystic Armor healers, and it can
be troublesome if they're your only healer. Monk eventually gets Renew
though, a MAG ignoring healing spell, so it's not a big deal.

### **Knight**

They have endgame healing and physical DPS, but they don't have early
game healing. However, unlike Monk, which takes Time Battlemage due in
large part to gaining Heavy Armor and Cura, Knight would like Mystic
Armor because boosted Excalibur is good (in the very endgame) and they
lack Renew, a MAG ignoring healing spell.

This makes Knight much trickier to build for comprehensive power, but
I'll go over each Mystic Armor option:

- Red Battlemage

  - Red Battlemage/Knight is a very comprehensively powerful option,
    with strong healing, buffs, and physical DPS throughout the game.

  - However, this combination only has Swiftness 1. Each rank of
    Swiftness is worth 10% Physical DPS when non-berserked and 5%
    Physical DPS when berserked. This is a little gross because they're
    reliant on Physical DPS in the endgame. (Because Berserk halves the
    charge time, it also halves DPS increase of both Swiftness and
    Haste.)

  - You can always respec Red Battlemage away in the endgame!

- Black Mage

  - Similar to Red Battlemage/Knight, but it trades in some early game
    healing for better magical damage throughout the entire game. This
    also provides an incredibly strong magical DPS option when using
    Mystic Armor and Shields. (Remember that when wearing Mystic Armor,
    magic will do more damage than even endgame weapons if the stat
    difference is at least 24, aka, wearing all Mystic Armor.)

  - This combination has Swiftness 2, which isn't nearly as gross as Red
    Battlemage/Knight, as they're not reliant on Physical DPS in the
    endgame while non-berserked.

- Bushi

  - The difference between this and Black Mage is that you trade in all
    of the Black Magic for Swiftness 3 in the endgame. Since they're
    significantly less powerful as a non-berserked DPS now (due to
    missing Black Magic for the entire game), their main advantage is
    that when Berserked, they have 5% more DPS. I don't think this is
    worth it if you only run 1 Knight (due to 12 jobs), but it's worth
    running if you run 2 Knights.

### **Spellcaster Gambits**

Spellcasters will not have everything in this list. This is more to show
a general gambit list to be taken from.

1.  Ally: HP \< X%: Strongest Healing Spell

2.  Ally: Any: Esuna

3.  Ally: Any: Raise/Arise

4.  DPS: Appropriate damage buffs (*Bravery, Faith, Berserk, Haste/ga*,
    etc)

5.  Ally: Any: Defensive buffs (*Protect/ga, Shell/ga)*

6.  Any Enemy: HP = 100%: Steal

7.  Spells that Exploit enemy weaknesses (this is optional, boosted
    spells often one-shot anyways)

8.  Attack or Strongest Magic Spell

9.  Self: Quality of Life buffs (*Libra*, *Float*)

10. Any Ally: Cura (This is to top off any party member that took damage
    after a fight.)

If you run multiple healers, you should decide which one should be your
primary healer and secondary healer.

#### **Main Healer or Secondary Healer?**

If you have multiple Spellcasters capable of healing in your party, then
you'll have to decide which character should be the main healer and
which one should be the secondary healer.

I like to have my main healer heal at 70% and my secondary healer heal
at 40%.

Generally speaking, the Spellcaster that's better at dealing damage
should be your secondary healer if you want to maximize for DPS.

If you want to maximize MP efficiency, then the Spellcaster that's
better at dealing damage should be your main healer (since dealing
damage *restores* MP).

# Intermediate Information and Context

The following section is mostly geared towards players who have
experience with Final Fantasy XII in some form, and is primarily focused
around doing difficult areas early for their incredible rewards.

A good test to see if this section is suitable for you is if you know
how to access *Nercohol of Nabudis* early, but even so, if you've read
the previous section, you should have enough knowledge to understand the
following concepts.

A lot of this information is about providing the context to understand
why specific, further optimizations have been made, since a lot of it is
pretty often misunderstood.

## Doing Difficult Areas Early

As you may have noticed, a lot of the early notable weapons in the table
are obtained from doing difficult areas early. For whatever reason,
Final Fantasy XII allows you to sequence break the optional areas to a
certain extent. This is pretty much the primary reason why you can
obtain strong weapons and magic early.

It's very interesting to note that the rewards for doing the difficult
areas early, even without completing the bosses in them, are often
significantly better than the hunts that you can do at the same time.

The main reason for further optimization is to do difficult areas early
more easily for their rewards, while preserving endgame strength. In
some sense, doing difficult areas early is more difficult than most of
the endgame content, because of the vast amount of reasons, both
equipment-wise and level-wise you have access to at the end of the game
as compared to the early game.

Of course, if you do not want to do difficult areas early, then the
entire rest of the guide is somewhat unnecessary.

But, if you do want to tackle difficult content early on, then continue
reading.

## The "Leader" Playstyle

This section covers why a playstyle centered around putting damage onto
1 character (generally also known as a "Tank") is so powerful.

### **On HP% Conditions**

In Final Fantasy XII, there are various augments and mechanics that
greatly increase a character's effectiveness if they meet one of the
various HP conditions:

- Focus/Serenity

  - **Focus multiplies your physical damage by 1.5x *when your HP is
    full.***

  - **Serenity multiples your magical damage and healing by 1.5x,** (and
    makes magical status effects 2.0x likely to hit) ***when your HP is
    full.***

- Adrenaline/Spellbreaker

  - **Adrenaline multiplies your physical damage by 2.0x *when your HP
    is less than 25%***

  - **Spellbreaker multiplies your magical damage by 2.0x, multiplies
    your healing by 1.5x** (I tested this, this was weird, and it also
    makes magical status effects 2.0x likely to hit) ***when your HP is
    less than 25%.***

- Physical Weapon Combo Rate

  - Most physical weapons in Final Fantasy XII have a chance to hit
    multiple times.

  - How often you hit multiple times (a "combo") is dependent on your
    weapon and if you're wearing the Genji Gloves.

  - **When your character combos, the number of hits that you do is
    dependent on your HP, with you doing more hits at lower HP.**

  - **Your combos are longest when your HP is less than 6.25%.**

  - **Combined with Adrenaline, Physical DPS can deal massive, massive
    amounts of damage.**

**These bonuses are massive, and multiplicatively stack with other
bonuses (like Bravery/Faith and Berserk). Focus/Serenity in particular
is almost always worth trying to maintain as it's fairly safe and easy
to maintain in a lot of situations.**

**Because of this, to maximize both healing and damage, you should try
to distribute as much damage as you can to 1 character in the party,
rather than having all characters take damage.**

**Of course, this isn't always possible, due to either AOE damage or
"reflected" damage. Nevertheless, there's no real opportunity cost for
building a team that's able to do so, so we might as well build a team
composition able to do so.**

As an example, let's consider Team Generalist. All of them will heal,
take damage, and deal damage. This time, for simplicity, the team's
damage output is 3.0x and healing output is 3.0x, 1.0x for each
character.

Let's consider if we distribute all of the damage onto 1 character and
leave the other characters at maximum HP. That character's effectiveness
will be 1.0x, but the other two character's effectiveness will be 1.5x
for damage and healing, giving a total of 3.0x damage and healing
output. **We've effectively gained another character's worth of
effectiveness by distributing all the damage onto 1 character.**

Of course, going even to even lower HP thresholds can net you even more
DPS, but it's tricky to maintain and stay alive, and the time that you
would save from doing so generally gets canceled out by the manual
menuing and control that you have to do to set it up. See [[4x
Speed]{.underline}](#x-speed) for a more in-depth discussion of this.
However, there are fights in which you would want to do this.

**Not all classes actually have all of these HP% augments that would be
relevant to them, so it's important to pair classes so that they'll have
these augments.**

Here's a table of what HP augments classes have:

+-----------------------+---------------------------+-----------------------+
| **Focus/Adrenaline**  | **Serenity/Spellbreaker** | **None**              |
+-----------------------+---------------------------+-----------------------+
| Uhlan                 | White Mage                | Machinist             |
|                       |                           |                       |
| Knight                | Black Mage                |                       |
|                       |                           |                       |
| Monk                  | Red Battlemage            |                       |
|                       |                           |                       |
| Foebreaker            | Time Battlemage\          |                       |
|                       | Bushi                     |                       |
| Shikari               |                           |                       |
|                       |                           |                       |
| Archer\*              |                           |                       |
+=======================+===========================+=======================+

\*For some reason, Archer is missing Adrenaline.

**In practice, however, most combinations will get you the relevant
augments, so you don't have to worry about this too much.**

The main ones to look out for are Archer, Bushi, and Machinist, as they
lack some physical HP augments, but deal physical damage. Avoid pairing
them together for physical DPS, but you should've already avoided doing
so because they're all "Fast" classes.

(All Dark Robe combos with Machinist + Dark Shot will be missing Focus
and Adrenaline, but because you can use your accessory slot to equip one
of these HP augments, it's not that big of a deal, since Guns have no
other damage increase accessory.)

The other ones to look out for is pairing Knight and Monk with a Mage
Class (or Bushi) if you want to heal with them because Serenity and
Spellbreaker can boost healing so much.

### **On Evasion**

It's a pretty common misconception that evasion is worthless because
hunts and bosses ignore it. Although this is partly true, most hunts and
bosses ignore physical evasion, this ignores that **normal enemies
almost never ignore evasion.**

**The reason why you'd want evasion against normal enemies is because if
you go to endgame areas early, evasion will greatly decrease the amount
of damage that you take, allowing you to much more easily complete the
area and pick up the treasure lying around.**

**Some of these endgame normal enemies have stats that equal, or even
exceed, the stats of hunts that you have access to at the same time.**

**It's also important to note that if your characters take less damage,
your characters will need less healing, so ultimately, they have more
time to deal damage instead. This is part of the reason why
survivability can *increase* your DPS.**

**Additionally, if you're trying to distribute all damage onto 1
character, it's obviously helpful if they can actually survive that
damage, and evasion can help with that.**

Of course, in the endgame, there's no more optional areas to do early,
and you have access to so many resources, in terms of gear, levels,
consumable items, so evasion becomes less important in the endgame, as
you can brute force it with enough time spent in the game.

But even in the endgame, there are uses for evasion, the primary one
being the Abysteel junction in Henne Mines part 2, the best spot for
level grinding.

# The Leader Role

With the background information out of the way, we can now define the
Leader role.

The Leader Role isn't so much of a specific role, so much as it's a plan
on where the damage should go, so the other party members can maintain
HP% augments.

Because of this, their only requirement is that they take the team's
damage, not die, and support the team as much as they can without the
expectation of maintaining HP% augments.

Generally, you want to lead the party with them, as enemies will often
attack the first enemy that they see. For this reason, it\'s best to
avoid Berserked Physical DPSs, as you cannot directly control them.

Since their main role is to not die and support the team as much as
possible, it's very, very natural to make Healers the Leader, and have
the other two party members double down on damage, whether magical or
physical, although strictly speaking, this isn't something that you have
to do.

With this in mind, I'll go over the best combinations for the Leader
role.

## Red Battlemage/Shikari (in the early to mid game)

Due to the focus on evasion early on, the best class in this regard is
Shikari, as they have access to *Main Gauche,* a dagger that does
somewhat poor damage, but has 50% evasion on it, and you can still wear
a shield with it! You can buy this weapon in *Jahara* (\~35% of the
game), so it's easy to get as well.

The best class to pair with Shikari while using *Main Gauche* is Red
Battlemage because they're the only single class that can heal and deal
magical damage (so they can ignore the poor attack rating on *Main
Gauche*).

(Black Mage will not get to use their elemental boosting staves while
using *Main Gauche*, so Red Battlemage doesn't lose much DPS as compared
to Black Mage, until Black Mage acquires non-elemental spells.)

### **On Shikari/White Mage**

This is also good, and is a better option than Red Battlemage/Shikari in
the endgame. Despite this, Shikari/White Mage is worse than Black
Mage/Knight or Monk/Time Battlemage in the endgame, so it's kind of a
wash.

Also, *Main Gauche* doesn't do very much damage, White Mage/Shikari will
tend to have MP issues while using *Main Gauche.* And in terms of a
damage dealer, Shikari will do less DPS than a spell slinging Red
Battlemage in the early and mid game, mostly due to wearing Mystic Armor
for healing. In the endgame, as a pure Physical DPS, they will also do
less DPS, due to the lack of Heavy Armor. (Red Battlemage has access to
endgame Heavy Armor via an Esper.)

### **On Fire Boosted Ardor**

It turns out that Firaga has similar DPS to Ardor. Although Ardor has
about 50% more power as compared to Firaga, against a single target,
Ardor has about an 80% longer animation time. After taking into account
charge time and defense, they deal similar DPS. (Ardor is better at
Reflect strategies as compared to Firaga.)

Burning Bow (and by extension, Dhanusha), will also out DPS Ardor when
Berserked if you want Fire damage specifically.

Both of these make pairing Red Battlemage with Archer or Black Mage
extremely awkward, as you do not actually get a real DPS increase from
either of these pairings.

Also, Physical DPS outdamages magic by far in the endgame, so the only
time you would actually want to use sustained Magical DPS is against
bosses that become immune to Physical (so, Chaos, Ultima, and Zodiark),
but all of them are either immune to Fire damage or use Shift (elemental
affinity scrambling), making Ardor fairly pointless.

## Knight/Black Mage (endgame only)

Knight/Black Mage is more or less the endgame version of Shikari/Red
Battlemage.

In the endgame, it's not so important that you use *Main Gauche*
specifically, as you gain access to good shields with 50 or more
evasion. From a theoretical perspective, not *as* tanky, but still
really tanky.

However, unlike Red Battlemage/Shikari, they will eventually have access
to endgame White Magic, including Bravery and Faith, allowing them to
take over as the evasion tank, as they keep up with endgame healing and
buffs.

They will also have non-elemental spells, incredibly useful to use as
you cannot use the elemental boosting staves (or *Burning Bow*) with
shields, as they are all two-handed, and being a Black Mage and all,
means that they can serve as one of the best Magical DPSs in the game.

In addition, they also serve as one of the best Physical DPSs in the
game, due to Knight being a good Physical DPS class and having access to
Mystic Armor (for Holy Boosted Excalibur.)

### **On Knight/Bushi**

Bushi has 3 Swiftness Lores, while Black Mage has 2. This is a 5%
Physical DPS difference while berserked, while Knight/Black Mage is so
much more versatile when non-berserked, especially before getting Tier 2
or higher weapons.

## Monk/Time Battlemage (more towards the mid and endgame)

This combination functions a little bit differently than the other two
combinations as a leader.

Although they have massive amounts of HP, due to their lesser amount of
evasion and worse healing, they generally aren't as durable as the other
two combinations (though still plenty durable if you play the game
normally).

The lack of Mystic Armor and Faith means that their healing will not be
as effective as the two characters for most of the game, although in the
endgame, this is more of a "theoretical" limit, rather than a practical
one, as Curaja restores a lot of health, and they will eventually get
the spell *Renew,* which just restores 100% of HP, regardless of MAG or
Faith.

They also won't reach the damage heights of Red Battlemage/Shikari in
the early game and Knight/Black Mage in the late game, although they are
still incredibly solid as a damage dealer throughout the game.

What they have instead is the best Physical DPS utility, as they're the
only class combination with Haste, Bravery, Expose (which reduces enemy
defense), Renew, and Reverse (the last two are used to set allied HP to
1).

What this means is that in terms of maximizing Physical DPS *as a team,*
no other combination comes close.

### **On Genji Gloves on Monk**

You may be wondering if Genji Gloves (so Knight/Foebreaker/Bushi) worth
it on Monk over Time Battlemage. The short answer is no, it's not.

It's worth noting for Monk that Haste contributes about 20% DPS for
non-berserked Physical DPSs, while Genji Gloves provides 25% DPS on
Kanya *only,* so a non-berserked, hasted Monk/Time Battlemage will have
similar individual DPS to a non-berserked, non-hasted, Genji Gloves
Monk.

By providing Haste to the entire team, however, they can easily make up
that missing 5%, so this combination increases your overall DPS more
than Monk/Genji Gloves while non-berserked.

Of course, before you have 99 STR with Genji Armor, Monk/Time Battlemage
will deal far more damage due to having access to better Heavy Armor,
and they have significantly better support throughout the entire game to
boot.

As a Genji Gloves Berserked DPS, Bushi and Knight are both generally
better options than Monk and Monk doesn't really contribute anything
particularly special to Bushi that Knight or Uhlan doesn't.

3.  Ally: Any: Raise/Arise

    etc)

6.  Ally: Any: Defensive buffs (*Protect/ga, Shell/ga)*

    case you run out of gambits)

9.  Attack or Strongest Magic Spell

11. Any Ally: Cura (This is to top off any party member that took damage
    during a fight to trigger Focus/Serenity.)

The main gambit difference between this and a Spellcaster is Self: Decoy
somewhere in the gambits.

### **Main Healer or Secondary Healer: Part 2**

Another thing that you should now consider is that if your Leader is
healing is whether they'll be the main healer or the secondary healer.

If the Leader is the main healer, they will not heal as much (due to HP%
augments), but your secondary healer will be able to do more damage.

If the Leader is the secondary healer, the secondary will not deal as
much damage (because they're healing instead), but your team will have
more healing.

In easy areas, I would lean towards making the Leader the main healer
for higher DPS, and in difficult areas, I would lean towards making the
Leader the secondary leader for better healing.

# Intermediate Team Combinations

You can just put all three of these leaders into your party because
there isn't actually any big opportunity cost to doing so. They're all
already almost optimal as DPSs go anyways, so you can just take all
three of them:

- Red Battlemage/Shikari

- Knight/Black Mage

- Monk/Time Battlemage

The final three characters can be whatever, really.

Characters that would be nice on top, for versatility:

- Another Healer (White Mage)

- Another Elemental Boosted Magical DPS (Black Mage)

- Physical DPSs to fill the rest of the characters.

White Mage/Black Mage conveniently covers the first two.

Bushi/Knight is another Physical DPS. Knight and Bushi both have really
good weapons. This Knight, however, isn't going to bother with any magic
spells from Espers and is just going to be permanently berserked.

Archer/Uhlan is also another Physical DPS. They're going to be
permanently Berserked, so anything past that doesn't matter. I like
taking Archer for their good item usage in emergencies and good weapon
progression, transitioning to Zodiac Spear in the endgame.

If you're running 12 jobs for some reason, I'd run White Mage/Machinist,
Bushi/Foebreaker, and Uhlan/Archer instead, but any variation of these
classes will be fine.

# Advanced Job Utility

In the Basic section, we ignored a lot of the extra utility that the
jobs can provide, especially the Physical ones.

This section will cover this utility. Although it's not usually very
important, I still wanted to at least mention it.

### 

## Archer

They have access to 3 Phoenix Lores, which means that their Phoenix
Downs heal for 50% Max HP. When combined with *Pheasant Netsuke* (after
*Raithwall, \~35%)*, an accessory that doubles the potency of items,
Phoenix Downs will restore 100% life.

Since all items have 0 CT, it means that you can revive characters to
100% before the enemy has the chance to act again if you set the battle
speed to its minimum (this is a setting in the options and is separate
from the entire game speed multiplier).

This actually makes Archer the strongest healer per CT, even stronger
than White Mage for the entire game, as long as you're willing to spam
Phoenix Downs, especially if you try to take on a fight that's out of
your league.

From my experience, no boss can actually outdamage Pheasant Netsuke +
Phoenix Down spam and it's easy enough to have 99 of them.

They also have Cura, but it's fairly irrelevant for most builds, as they
are usually permanently berserked (so they can't cast spells). Even if
they weren't berserked, they don't have a very good MAG stat unless they
were paired with Bushi or something, so it's not really worth building
around, especially when Time Battlemage and Red Battlemage also provide
Cura but significantly more utility as a spellcaster.

## Black Mage

Status effects are actually useful in Final Fantasy XII. A lot of normal
enemies and even hunts can be inflicted with incredibly debilitating
status effects. Black Mage is excellent at inflicting status effects,
especially with *Indigo Pendant* (\~60% at the earliest), which
basically ensures that all status effects have a 100% chance to be
inflicted.

## Shikari

They have *Main Gauche,* which provides +50% evasion. Very useful. They
also have access to a good amount of guns for some reason.

## Red Battlemage

It's sort of an exaggeration to say they cannot heal effectively in the
endgame. 7 out of the 12 classes have access to either *Demon Shield* or
*Black Mask,* both of which allow the wearer to absorb Dark damage. This
puts their healing on par with Curaja if you use this strategy.

However, this comes at a significant DPS penalty for most characters
(due to having to wear Mystic Armor or a Shield).

This still doesn't make up for the fact that they're missing endgame
buffs.

Their Time Magic utility will be covered under Time Battlemage.

## Time Battlemage

Again, they have access to status effects, although more niche ones as
compared to Black Mage, but they still can be useful.

Reflect can be useful for maximizing Magical DPS. You put reflect onto
all of your characters and cast AOE spells on yourself. This essentially
multi-casts the spell! This is kind of a hassle though because it makes
using healing spells difficult (they will also be reflected), but it can
be incredibly strong if you're willing to pull it out.

Balance is a spell that deals damage based off of the difference between
your current HP and your max HP. If you use Infuse with it (from another
character), you can reduce your HP to 10 fairly easily. Because you can
get this before Raithwall, this can be an incredibly damaging spell
throughout the game, but it's also kind of a hassle to set up.

(Time Battlemage has both Reflect and Balance, but not the next two
spells.)

Warp can instantly kill most normal enemies in an AOE (with 100% chance
too if you use *Indigo Pendant*), but you receive no EXP or LP.

Float is a really, really nice quality of life spell, allowing you to
ignore traps. It's kind of difficult to get your party members to avoid
traps and there are some locations (like the entrances to the Necrohol
of Nabudis) where they're unavoidable.

Stepping on a trap won't cause a game over, but they can be incredibly
annoying, like ones that inflict Disease on your characters or the ones
that reduce your MP to 0. In terms of quality of life, this is actually
more important than Haste.

## Machinist

They have measures, which are fun little ways to gain buffs without
spending MP. Unfortunately, the cost is that you have to manually menu
to gain their buffs. However, they're 1 handed and give 35 evasion,
which is quite good. Worse than Shikari, but still pretty good.

They can also get Hastega really late in the game via Esper. Hastega
takes a lot of MP though, and you get this really, really late.

## Foebreaker

They have access to all 4 stat breaking techniques. Every boss is
vulnerable to them and you can use them to reduce an enemy's damage and
defense to 0, allowing you trivally beat the fight, since they'll do 0
damage.

You get the damage reducing ones late in the game though.

Unfortunately, other classes also have them, especially Monk/Time
Battlemage.

Their endgame weapon can compete with the other best weapons (like
Tournesol, Zodiac Spear, and Kumbha) in DPS if you reduce the enemy's
defense to 0. They're very defense sensitive.

## Bushi

Shades of Black can be useful in the midgame for hitting flying enemies,
but a Berserked Physical DPS is usually better.

# Armor Bonuses

## The Damage Formula

First, let's have a look at the general damage and healing formula:

$DMG\  = \ (ATK*rand(1,1.25) - DEF)*(1 + MAIN*(LVL + SUB)/CONST)$

Here's what each term means, if you care:

+-----------------------+---------------------------------------------------+
| **Term**              | **Description**                                   |
+-----------------------+---------------------------------------------------+
| *ATK*                 | The strength of the weapon or spell.              |
+-----------------------+---------------------------------------------------+
| *DEF*                 | The defense of the enemy.                         |
+-----------------------+---------------------------------------------------+
| *rand(1,1.25)*        | This is a random number in the range of (1,       |
|                       | 1.125).                                           |
+-----------------------+---------------------------------------------------+
| *MAIN*                | The main stat of the weapon or spell.             |
|                       |                                                   |
|                       | For most weapons, this is STR.                    |
|                       |                                                   |
|                       | For magic, this is MAG.                           |
+-----------------------+---------------------------------------------------+
| *LVL*                 | This is the level of your character.              |
+-----------------------+---------------------------------------------------+
| *SUB*                 | The secondary stat of the weapon or spell.        |
|                       |                                                   |
|                       | For most weapons, this is also STR.               |
|                       |                                                   |
|                       | Some weapons instead use MAG, VIT, or SPD.        |
|                       |                                                   |
|                       | For magic, this is also MAG.                      |
+-----------------------+---------------------------------------------------+
| *CONST*               | This is a constant to normalize the damage.       |
|                       |                                                   |
|                       | For STR and MAG weapons and all magic, this is    |
|                       | 256.                                              |
|                       |                                                   |
|                       | For SPD weapons, this is 218.                     |
|                       |                                                   |
|                       | For VIT weapons, this is 128. In VIT's case       |
|                       | though, the random function is in the range of    |
|                       | (0, 1) instead.                                   |
+=======================+===================================================+

If we look at this damage formula, notice that there's two parts being
multiplied together: the contextual parts, which are based on the weapon
and enemy, and the character part, which is based on their stats.

Notice that for any particular weapon and enemy combination, we can view
differences in strength by looking only at the character stat part,
which is what I'll do in the next table.

Different characters, even between the males and females, have somewhat
different base stats, but it's not super significant. The real stat
differential, however, is when you take into armor.

Before we can compute the relative effectiveness, we need to know about
how much of a stat characters have at a particular level and equipment
combination.

Raw Stat Methodology:

- Base stats were computed by taking the average of every character's
  STR and MAG.

- Stat per Piece was obtained by looking up about the appropriate
  equipment for a character's level. From level 1 to 40, this is just
  store bought equipment.

- Lores, which are the Battle Lores and Magic Lores that a job can
  obtain, add +1 to either STR or MAG. For simplicity, I took it to be
  10, which is about the average for a class that uses that stat. At
  Level 1 and Level 20, it's unlikely that you actually have all of them
  (they cost LP), so I reduced their amount.

### **Stats at each level, depending on equipment**

With the stats at each level, we can compute their stat coefficient (the
part of the damage formula based upon their stats) in the general case
where the main stat and the sub stat are the same and the constant is
256.

Then, we can normalize each coefficient by dividing each by the amount
in the "0 Armor Pieces" column for their row.

### **Normalized Stat Coefficients**

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

                    Pieces**                            Pieces**
  ----------------- ----------------- ----------------- -----------------

  20                1.00              1.21              1.45

This is because Foebreaker's weapon is incredibly DEF reliant due to
their damage formula. Roughly speaking, compared to other weapons, it's:

(ATK**\*random(0, 1.111)** - DEF) \* (STATS **\* 2**)

random(0, 1.111) can be substituted with about 0.5, so after
distributing the 2 on the right, we get:

(ATK - **2\***DEF) \* (STATS)

This is an approximation, but it's a useful one to see why Foebreaker
gains so much damage when defense is 0, which is amusing considering
that their ultimate weapon is a hammer.

Ultimately, this makes Vrsicika a Tier 1 Weapon, when at 0 DEF.

# 4x Speed

The ability to use 4x speed most of the time, as it's literally the
**SINGLE** largest DPS increase in the game.

Yes, in *aggregate*, you can increase your DPS past 4x. **SINGLE** is
the word here for a reason.

This may sound sort of silly, but there are circumstances in which you
can't use 4x Speed, the primary one being: **menuing.**

What this means is that if you have to menu to do things, you've lost a
lot of DPS. Here's reasons why you may have to menu and lose DPS:

- On 4x Speed, you also take 4x damage.

  - If your team dies you'll obviously need to menu to swap something in
    or redo content.

  - If your team isn't surviviable enough to play without 4x because you
    need to carefully watch what's going on, you've also lost a lot of
    DPS.

- **Getting your party members to extremely low HP to do extremely high
  damage is generally pretty awkward to do with gambits alone until you
  reach end-game, and is not worth it for the vast majority of fights in
  this game, especially if your characters die!**

- Syphoning off MP from a character that has excess MP. This is very,
  very relevant if you have a healer that doesn't regenerate MP on their
  own. This is why I always pair main healers with the ability to deal
  damage, because damage translates into MP once you get a passive.

- Changing up your gambits or equipment to use a specific strategy or
  hit a specific weakness (although this takes less time with the
  introduction of gambit sets.)

  - Reflect strategies would be the quintessential version of this.

  - Changing your elemental boosting weapon to match the enemy's
    weakness

  - Changing your DPS's accessories to achieve higher in-game DPS, like
    swapping between Genji Gloves and Cameo Belt often.

- If you want to take advantage of the ability to use items and cast
  spells on non-active party members, you need to do that by menuing.

**There are several points to this:**

- **There are *specific* strategies that might have *higher in-game
  DPS*, but ultimately have *lower real-time DPS* because of the
  unavailability of 4x speed for that strategy (like heavy menuing).**

- **The primary way this can occur by manually reducing your HP for high
  DPS strategies. Even ignoring that you're at low HP and might die, I
  don't usually think it's worth the *real world time* to do this, since
  the vast majority of fights are so short anyways!**

- **Thus, I think it's silly to entirely evaluate a weapon's performance
  based on what they can do in 5% HP circumstances, since that
  encompasses so little of the weapon's use and doing such a strategy
  isn't usually faster than "lol 4x Speed" with weapons in normal use!**

**Yes, there are caveats to this:**

- There are fights in which 4x Speed doesn't help (like the Demon Wall
  fights). When this is the case, yes, it is more important to maximize
  *in-game* DPS than *real-world* DPS!

- There are fights in which you'd want to maximize your theoretical DPS
  (like Hell Wrym and Yiazmat)! I'm not saying they don't exist, but
  that they're incredibly limited scenarios.
